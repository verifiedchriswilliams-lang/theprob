## 1. One-Liner

The Prob is a fully automated prediction market intelligence platform that tracks live crowd probabilities across Kalshi and Polymarket, surfaces the most interesting price moves every day, and delivers them as a daily email newsletter.

---

## 2. The Elevator Pitch (spoken)

The Prob is my answer to a question I kept asking myself: what does the crowd actually think is going to happen, in real money terms? Prediction markets are basically a live crowd-sourced probability engine, and I wanted a way to read that signal every morning without drowning in noise. So I built a platform that monitors over 2,000 live markets across Kalshi and Polymarket, scores them for buzz and relevance, and packages the most interesting ones into a daily newsletter and a live website. Everything runs automatically. No human edits. The pipelines fire every hour, the newsletter builds itself at 6:50am, and a real trading bot places real bets on Kalshi using the same signals the site surfaces. It is one of those projects where building it was just as interesting as using it.

---

## 3. The Problem and the Why

Prediction markets are genuinely useful for understanding what informed, money-on-the-line crowds believe about the future. But following them is tedious. You have to visit Polymarket, visit Kalshi, mentally reconcile their different formats and scales, figure out which markets are actually moving, and try to read signal from 2,000+ simultaneous bets. Nobody has time for that.

The existing options were basically just the raw platforms themselves. There was no product designed for a reader who wants prediction market intelligence the way they want market intelligence from Bloomberg or the way they get sports scores: fast, curated, with an editorial point of view on what matters.

I built The Prob to be the daily brief for that reader. The value proposition is not raw data access. It is curation, synthesis, and a consistent voice that can say "here is what the crowd moved 9 points today, here is where Polymarket and Kalshi disagree by 14 points, here is what both of those things probably mean."

## 4. What It Actually Does

**The website (theprob.ai)**

When you land on theprob.ai you get a live market feed updated every hour. The top of the page shows the Market of the Day: one question, one big probability number in green or red, a 24-hour price change, total trading volume, and a short editorial take written by the platform in a voice that sounds like a trading desk crossed with The Hustle newsletter. Beneath that is a movers table showing the 5 or 6 markets that moved the most in the past 24 hours, each with a probability and a direction chip. Below that is a section called The Spread, which shows Polymarket vs. Kalshi pricing on the same event side by side, highlighting where the two platforms disagree by 8 points or more. The site covers multiple categories: Politics, Business, Tech, Sports, Culture, and World. There are dedicated category pages for each.

**The newsletter**

Every morning (Monday through Saturday, 6:50am ET, with a Sunday edition too) a daily newsletter builds itself and sends to subscribers. The subject line and editorial copy are generated by Claude, the AI model from Anthropic. The newsletter leads with a hero market, shows today's movers, surfaces The Spread comparisons, curates 3 relevant news articles with AI-written two-sentence summaries, and closes with The Prob's Take, an editorial opinion on the most interesting market of the day. The newsletter is sent via Resend from newsletter@theprob.ai.

**The Portfolio Experiment**

One of the more distinctive features is the Portfolio page. I run paper trading experiments, which are simulated portfolios that use the platform's own signals to pick one trade per day. Each experiment tracks multiple models with different strategies, starting each with a hypothetical $1,000 balance and measuring returns over a defined period. The results are live on portfolio.html, including a full trade ledger. Experiment 1 (Test 2, March through June 2026) ran three models for 83 days. Model A, called The Crowd, returned +99.6% on 383 winning trades. Model B, called The Contrarian, returned +93.1% on 69 trades. Model C, called The Arb, never got off the ground and ended at 0% because the arbitrage strategy it used proved too fragile to execute against the available data.

Experiment 2 launched October 1, 2026, with three new models: A2 (The Crowd, the control, same 65/35 probability gate as before), D (The Sharp Crowd, tighter 75/25 gate), and E (The Momentum Crowd, which requires both a 65/35 probability AND a 3-point directional move in the past 24 hours). The experiment runs through October 31 and trades resolve through mid-November.

**The live trading bot**

Beyond paper trading, there is a real live bot running on Kalshi with real money. It runs every hour via GitHub Actions, sizes each trade at 10% of the current account balance, caps positions at 4 open trades simultaneously, stops if the account drops below $5 or if it is down $20 or more on the day, and only targets markets resolving within 7 days. The bot uses the same market scoring signals as the website.

## 5. How It Works Under the Hood

### For a smart non-engineer

Every hour, GitHub Actions (a free automated task runner) wakes up and runs Python scripts. Those scripts call the Polymarket and Kalshi APIs, pull live market data for thousands of questions, score each one for interest and relevance using a custom algorithm, and write the results to a JSON file that the website reads. The same data powers the newsletter, the portfolio tracker, and the trading bot. The whole thing lives on GitHub Pages, which serves the static HTML for free. There is no traditional server and no database. Everything that needs to persist goes into JSON files committed back to the repository.

### Tech stack

- **Language:** Python 3.11 (all pipeline scripts)
- **Frontend:** Static HTML, CSS (design-tokens.css), vanilla JavaScript. No framework.
- **Hosting:** GitHub Pages (theprob.ai via CNAME)
- **Storage:** Git repository as the database. All data lives in the `data/` folder as JSON files committed by GitHub Actions bots.
- **Automation:** GitHub Actions. Five workflows: fetch-markets (hourly), fetch-news (4x daily), run-bot (hourly, offset by 5 minutes), send-newsletter (Mon-Sat 6:50am ET, paused pending Resend verification), send-sunday5 (Sunday 6:50am ET, paused).
- **AI / LLMs:** Anthropic Claude API.
  - `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` in fetch_markets.py for the hero market "prob take" blurb and the daily editorial take. Fast and cheap for high-frequency generation.
  - `claude-sonnet-4-20250514` in fetch_news.py for two-sentence news summaries.
  - `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` in send_newsletter.py for the subject line and inbox preview text.
  - All prompts embed the house style guide inline: no em dashes, short sentences, active voice, confident and not hedging, undetectable as AI.
- **Email delivery:** Resend (resend.com). Sends from newsletter@theprob.ai. The domain theprob.ai is verified with DKIM and SPF via GoDaddy DNS. Previously used Beehiiv, migrated to Resend in late September 2026.
- **Subscriber list:** 29 active subscribers stored as a JSON array in a GitHub Actions secret.
- **Market data sources:** Polymarket (via their public CLOB API) and Kalshi (via their authenticated Trade API v2, requiring RSA key signing on every request).
- **News source:** Google News RSS, queried with three search terms: "Polymarket prediction market," "Kalshi prediction market," and "prediction markets news."
- **Trending topics:** Wikipedia trending articles API used to add a bonus to markets that match current real-world trending topics.
- **Dependencies:** `requests`, `cryptography` (for Kalshi RSA auth). No other third-party libraries in the pipeline scripts.

### The scoring algorithm (how markets get ranked)

The core of the product is `score_market()` in fetch_markets.py. It assigns every market a composite buzz score using these signals, weighted roughly in this order:

1. Absolute price move in the past 24 hours (big moves = breaking news)
2. 24-hour trading volume (money rushing in is the best real-time signal of interest)
3. Total contract volume (legitimacy and liquidity)
4. Conviction probability (markets near 65% or higher are more actionable and interesting)
5. Days until resolution (urgency bonus for markets closing soon)
6. Recency surge (ratio of 24h volume to total volume)
7. Trade eligibility (bonus for markets that also qualify for the paper portfolio)
8. Polymarket "featured" flag (+6 points)
9. Kalshi bid-ask spread tightness (tight spread = liquid = more trustworthy signal)
10. Trending topic match vs. Wikipedia trends (+2 per match, capped at +8)
11. US audience relevance (+4 for US politics/sports/companies, +2 for global topics)
12. Resolution distance penalty (markets resolving more than 30 days out are penalized; more than 180 days out get -4 points)

The hero market (Market of the Day) runs a separate selection pass on top of this that also enforces category diversity, a minimum volume gate ($250,000 total or $500,000 24h), and deduplication against the previous day's hero to avoid repetition.

### The paper portfolio trade picker

Each model uses a different gate on the same market data:

- **Model A / A2 (The Crowd):** Pick the highest-scoring market where probability is >= 65% (YES trade) or <= 35% (NO trade), resolving within 14 days.
- **Model B (The Contrarian, Experiment 1 only):** Pick the highest-scoring market where probability is <= 35% for a YES trade (betting against the crowd).
- **Model C (The Arb, Experiment 1 only):** Look for the same market on both platforms with a gap >= 5 points. Never worked reliably due to data matching fragility.
- **Model D (The Sharp Crowd):** Same as Model A but tighter gates: >= 75% or <= 25%.
- **Model E (The Momentum Crowd):** >= 65% or <= 35%, AND the market must have moved 3 or more points in the trade direction in the past 24 hours.

Trades close when the probability reaches >= 95% (win for YES) or <= 5% (loss for YES), or when the market's end date passes (settled at the majority side of 50%).

### Infrastructure

- **Deployment:** git push to main branch. GitHub Pages serves automatically from the root.
- **Domain:** theprob.ai (GoDaddy registrar, GoDaddy DNS).
- **Environments:** One. There is no staging environment. All changes go to production via the main branch.
- **Secrets:** Stored in GitHub Actions repository secrets. Names: ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, KALSHI_KEY_ID, KALSHI_PRIVATE_KEY, RESEND_API_KEY, SUBSCRIBER_LIST. (Previously also BEEHIIV_API_KEY, BEEHIIV_PUB_ID, LOOPS_API_KEY, LOOPS_TRANSACTIONAL_ID from earlier platform attempts.)
- **Cost:** GitHub Actions free tier handles all compute. Anthropic API (pay per token, Haiku is cheap). Resend free tier (3,000 emails/month, 100/day). GitHub Pages free. GoDaddy domain registration annual fee.

### Security and privacy

- No user accounts. No personal data collected in the application code.
- Kalshi API authentication uses RSA key signing. The private key is stored as a GitHub secret, never committed to the repo. (Note: a `kalshi_trading_key.pem` file exists at the repo root but should be verified to contain only a public key or non-sensitive material.)
- The subscriber email list is stored as a GitHub Actions secret, not in the codebase.

## 6. Design and Product Decisions

**"The Clean Book" design direction.** The current visual design was the winner of a three-way exploration (Clean Book vs. Broadsheet vs. Terminal). The other two directions are preserved in the repo as 01-broadsheet.html and 03-terminal.html but were not built out. The Clean Book direction was chosen for its editorial fintech feel: Instrument Serif for headlines, Instrument Sans for data, Geist Mono for labels.

**The % mark.** The percent sign is the entire brand. It means "What's the Probability." It is the favicon, the section eyebrow, the editorial sign-off, and the icon. The brand color (Ultramarine #3A2BD4) is never used for YES/NO signals. Green is reserved for up/YES and red for down/NO. This is a hard rule that prevents color confusion.

**No em dashes, ever.** This is baked into every AI prompt across all three scripts as an explicit instruction. The VOICE.md and HANDOFF.md both call it out as a hard rule. It appears to be a personal stylistic commitment that predates the AI-generated copy.

**No database, no server.** The entire product is static HTML plus JSON files in a git repo. This was a deliberate simplicity choice. GitHub Pages is free and infinitely scalable for a read-only site. The tradeoff is that all "state" (portfolio data, subscriber list, market data) lives in files and is managed by commit bots.

**Migration from Beehiiv to Resend.** The original newsletter infrastructure used Beehiiv (a newsletter platform), then Loops (a transactional email platform), before settling on Resend. The Beehiiv API started returning 403 errors. Resend was chosen for its clean REST API, free tier, and first-class Python SDK. As of late September 2026, the migration is complete but the auto-send schedule is paused pending final verification of the template and delivery.

**Experiment 1 lessons.** Model C (The Arb) was a failure because spread-matching across two platforms proved too fragile: the data structures were different enough that qualifying pairs almost never appeared. The lesson applied to Experiment 2 was to use only signals already proven reliable in the existing data pipeline, and to pick a hard end date (October 31) so the experiment actually concludes rather than drifting indefinitely.

## 9. Glossary

**Market of the Day (hero market):** The single highest-scoring prediction market question surfaced each day, displayed prominently at the top of the site and newsletter.

**The Prob's Take:** A 1-2 sentence editorial opinion generated by Claude on the day's hero market. Written to sound like a sharp human analyst, not a press release.

**The Spread:** A newsletter and website section that shows the same question priced differently on Polymarket and Kalshi, highlighting the gap. A 14-point gap means one platform has the event at 68% and the other at 54%.

**Movers:** Markets that moved the most in the past 24 hours, measured in percentage points. The daily movers section shows the top 5-6.

**Buzz score:** The composite numerical score assigned to every market by score_market(). It drives all ranking and selection decisions. Not shown to users but central to everything.

**Paper portfolio / Portfolio Experiment:** A simulated trading experiment where the platform picks one real market per day and tracks a hypothetical $1,000 balance as if $100 were bet on each pick. Results are public at theprob.ai/portfolio.html.

**Model A / The Crowd:** The baseline portfolio model. Bets YES on markets where the crowd probability is >= 65%, or NO where it is <= 35%.

**Model D / The Sharp Crowd:** A tighter version of Model A, requiring >= 75% or <= 25%.

**Model E / The Momentum Crowd:** Model A's gate plus a requirement that the market moved 3+ points in the trade direction in the last 24 hours. Conviction plus fresh momentum.

**The Contrarian (Model B):** Experiment 1 model that bet against the crowd (YES on low-probability markets). Returned +93.1%.

**The Arb (Model C):** Experiment 1 model that tried to exploit price gaps between Polymarket and Kalshi. Never executed a single trade. Retired.

**The Prob's Take eyebrow:** The recurring `% Section Name` pill-shaped label in Geist Mono that opens every newsletter and site section. The % is the brand mark.

**The Clean Book:** The name for The Prob's current design direction. Editorial fintech aesthetic. Won a three-way design exploration in mid-2026.

**Experiment 1 / Experiment 2:** Time-bounded paper portfolio tests, each running a set of trading models for one month (Experiment 2) or longer (Experiment 1). Used to compare strategies empirically.

**Resend:** The email infrastructure platform used to send the newsletter. Replaced Beehiiv in late September 2026.

## 10. Likely Questions and Good Answers

**What is The Prob?**
The Prob is a daily prediction market intelligence platform I built. It tracks over 2,000 live markets across Kalshi and Polymarket, scores them for buzz and relevance, and delivers the most interesting price moves every morning as a newsletter and a live website. The whole thing is fully automated. No human curates it. The data pipelines run every hour.

**How do I access it?**
The website is at theprob.ai. There is also a daily email newsletter. You can subscribe through the site.

**Is it free?**
Yes, completely free. The site, the newsletter, everything.

**What are prediction markets?**
Prediction markets are essentially betting exchanges where people put real money on the outcome of future events. If you think there is a 70% chance Anthropic will IPO this year, you can buy contracts at 70 cents that pay $1 if it happens. The price is a live crowd consensus. It is more accurate than polls because people have financial skin in the game.

**Why build this?**
I wanted a Bloomberg Terminal for prediction markets. A way to read the crowd signal every morning without visiting two different platforms, normalizing their formats, and figuring out which of 2,000 markets actually matter today. The existing options were the raw platforms themselves. I wanted the editorial layer on top. *(Full personal motivation: needs Chris.)*

**What makes the hero market selection work?**
There is a custom scoring algorithm called score_market() that weighs about a dozen signals: how much the probability moved in the last 24 hours, how much money traded in the last 24 hours, whether the market is resolving soon, whether it matches current trending topics from Wikipedia, and whether it is US-relevant. The hero market is the highest scorer that also meets a volume threshold and was not the hero the day before.

**What is The Spread?**
It is a section that shows where Polymarket and Kalshi price the same event differently. If Polymarket has the Anthropic IPO at 68% and Kalshi has it at 54%, that is a 14-point gap. One of them is wrong. Or there is genuine information asymmetry between the two platforms' user bases. Either way, it is interesting. The Spread surfaces those gaps when they hit 8 points or more.

**How does the newsletter get written?**
Almost everything is automated. The market data pipeline scores and selects the hero and movers. Claude generates the editorial take and the subject line. News articles are fetched from Google News RSS and summarized in two sentences by Claude. The HTML is assembled and sent automatically at 6:50am ET. I do not edit it before it goes out.

**What AI models does it use?**
Two Claude models from Anthropic. Claude Haiku for high-frequency tasks like the hero's editorial take, the daily take, and the newsletter subject line. Claude Sonnet for the higher-quality two-sentence news summaries. Both are prompted with a house style guide that enforces the voice: confident, no hedging, no em dashes, short active sentences.

**Tell me about the portfolio experiment.**
I run paper trading portfolios using the platform's own signals. Each model gets a hypothetical $1,000 and picks one trade per day based on different probability thresholds. In Experiment 1 (83 days, March-June 2026), Model A (called The Crowd) returned +99.6% and Model B (The Contrarian) returned +93.1%. Model C (an arbitrage strategy) never made a single trade because the cross-platform spread matching turned out to be too fragile in practice. Experiment 2 started October 1 with three new models testing tighter gates and momentum confirmation.

**Is there a real trading bot?**
Yes. There is a live bot running on Kalshi with real money. It runs every hour, sizes each trade at 10% of the current account balance, holds a maximum of 4 open positions, and targets only markets resolving within 7 days. It has a daily loss limit of $20. It uses the same market scoring signals as the website. *(Current balance and live P&L: needs Chris.)*

**Why only Kalshi and Polymarket?**
Those are the two largest US-accessible prediction markets with reliable APIs. Polymarket is the global volume leader. Kalshi is US-regulated with broader event categories. Together they cover the vast majority of interesting prediction market activity accessible to a US-based builder.

**How is the data stored?**
There is no traditional database. Everything is JSON files in a GitHub repository. The market data, news articles, portfolio state, and trade ledger are all committed back to the repo by automated bots after each pipeline run. GitHub Pages serves the static site directly from those files.

**What is the tech stack?**
Python for all the backend pipelines, static HTML plus vanilla JavaScript for the frontend, GitHub Actions for scheduling, GitHub Pages for hosting, and the Anthropic API for AI generation. No frameworks, no server, no database in the traditional sense. The whole thing could be rebuilt by a single engineer in a few days.

**How often does the data update?**
Market data updates every hour. News summaries update four times a day. The newsletter builds and sends once a day, Monday through Sunday.

**What does the % mark mean?**
It is the brand mark. It stands for "What's the Probability." It is the favicon, the section eyebrow label, and the editorial sign-off. The whole product is fundamentally about probability, so the percent sign is the right symbol.

**Is the newsletter actually good?**
I think so. It is written to sound like a trading desk crossed with The Hustle newsletter. Short sentences, real takes, no hedging. The AI is prompted to write as a sharp human with skin in the game, not as an assistant summarizing a press release. The no-em-dash rule is one of those stylistic commitments that sounds weird until you start noticing how much corporate writing leans on em dashes to avoid having a point of view.

**What has not worked?**
Model C (the arbitrage model) was a complete failure. The idea was sound: find the same event priced at a 5-point gap on both platforms and trade the cheaper side. The execution broke because matching the same event across two platforms with different data structures proved too fragile. Zero qualifying pairs ever showed up. I retired it after Experiment 1 ended.

The newsletter also went through two email infrastructure platforms before finding one that worked. Beehiiv started returning API errors. Loops had its own issues. Resend (the current platform) has a clean API, full domain verification, and a free tier that easily handles the current subscriber count.

**Who is it for?**
People who care about what is actually going to happen in politics, business, tech, sports, and culture, and prefer crowd consensus with real money behind it over punditry. Traders who want an edge. Journalists who want a data source. Curious people who like probability. *(More precise ICP: needs Chris.)*

**What is next?**
Experiment 2 runs through October. After that I will analyze which model performs best across a wider range of market categories, and likely build Experiment 3. On the product side, individual market detail pages and email subscriber growth are the obvious next things. *(Chris should confirm priorities.)*

**Is this just a Polymarket or Kalshi dashboard?**
No. Those platforms show their own data in their own formats. The Prob's value is the editorial layer: scoring, selection, comparison across platforms, and a voice that has an opinion about what the numbers mean. The Spread section in particular has no equivalent on either platform. And the portfolio experiments are original research.

**How many subscribers does the newsletter have?**
About 29 active subscribers right now. This is very early stage. The focus has been on building the product right before driving growth.
