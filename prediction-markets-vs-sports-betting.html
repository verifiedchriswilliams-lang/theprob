How They Work

A sportsbook sets the lines. A prediction market finds them. That's the whole difference.

Both involve money on outcomes. But the structure, purpose, and edge cases are fundamentally different. Here's how to think about each one.

Published December 8, 2026

The core structural difference

A sportsbook is a market maker. It sets the odds, takes bets from both sides, adjusts lines to balance its book, and collects vig (the spread between odds on each side). The house always has an edge built in.

A prediction market is a peer-to-peer exchange. No house sets the lines. Participants trade contracts with each other, and prices emerge from that trading. The platform collects a fee on transactions, but it doesn't take a position on the outcome.

The practical effect: in a sportsbook, you're betting against an odds-maker. In a prediction market, you're betting against other participants who disagree with you.

Reading the prices differently

Sportsbook odds include the vig. A game priced at -110 on both sides means you need to bet $110 to win $100. The implied probability of each side adds up to more than 100%. That gap is the book's margin.

Prediction market prices are closer to true probability estimates, because traders are competing against each other, not against the house. If a market prices YES at $0.68, that's a rough 68% probability signal — before fees. The vig equivalent is typically lower than a sportsbook's.

Illustrative. Converting between sportsbook odds and prediction market probabilities requires accounting for the vig.

What prediction markets cover that sportsbooks don't

Sportsbooks exist primarily to price sporting events. Prediction markets price anything that can be objectively resolved: election outcomes, economic data releases, geopolitical events, company decisions, weather events. For sports coverage specifically, see The Prob's sports markets.

Illustrative. Prediction markets span a much wider range of event categories than sports books.

When to use which

If you want to bet on a sports outcome and you have a strong edge on the spread, a sportsbook is designed for exactly that. High liquidity, fast markets, familiar interface.

If you want a probability signal for a non-sports event, or if you want to exit your position before resolution, prediction markets are more flexible. The ability to trade out of a position at any time — without waiting for the event to conclude — is a meaningful structural advantage for anyone who updates their views frequently.

Frequently asked questions

Are prediction markets the same as sports betting?
No. Sports betting involves a house that sets odds and takes the other side. Prediction markets are peer-to-peer exchanges where prices emerge from participant trading, and they cover far more than sports.
Which has lower fees?
Prediction markets typically have lower implied fees than traditional sportsbooks. Sportsbooks embed a vig of roughly 4-10%. Prediction market fees are transaction-based and usually lower, though this varies by platform and trade size.
Can I sell a prediction market contract before it resolves?
Yes. That's one of the structural advantages. If your position changes before the event, you can sell at the current market price rather than waiting for resolution.
Are prediction markets legal in the US?
Kalshi is CFTC-regulated and open to US users. Other platforms vary. Regulation in this space is active and evolving. Always verify current platform terms before trading.
