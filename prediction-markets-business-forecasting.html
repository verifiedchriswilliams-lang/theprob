Applied Forecasting

Your team won't tell you what they really think. A prediction market will.

Internal prediction markets solve a problem every organization has: getting honest probability estimates from people who know the work but have political reasons to hedge. Here's how they're actually used.

Published December 22, 2026

The forecasting gap in organizations

Most organizational forecasts are polluted by incentive problems. Project timelines get padded by teams that fear being held to aggressive deadlines. Sales forecasts get sandbagged or over-inflated depending on who benefits from which direction. Senior leaders get the numbers their reports think they want to hear.

The information exists. It lives in the engineers who know the technical debt is worse than the roadmap suggests, in the account managers who know which deals are real, in the ops team who can see the inventory gap coming. It just doesn't surface through standard planning channels.

An internal prediction market is a mechanism for surfacing it. For background on why market prices tend to aggregate information well, see are prediction markets accurate.

How internal prediction markets work

An organization sets up a market with binary questions relevant to its operations: "Will we ship feature X by [date]?" "Will Q3 revenue exceed $Y?" "Will the acquisition close before [date]?" Employees trade contracts using internal play money or small real-money stakes.

The price that emerges reflects the aggregate belief of people with direct information — not what they said in a planning meeting, but what they're willing to put behind. The gap between the official plan and the market price is the forecasting signal.

Illustrative. When official plans and market prices diverge, the market is usually reflecting information the planning process didn't surface.

Who actually uses them

Google ran internal prediction markets for years under the name "Prediction Markets" program, covering product launches, quarterly metrics, and competitive events. Hewlett-Packard ran internal markets for printer sales forecasting in research published in the early 2000s, finding that market prices outperformed official sales forecasts.

Firms like Metaculus and Cultivate Labs offer enterprise prediction market platforms. The U.S. Intelligence Community has also used prediction market approaches internally, most famously through the IARPA-funded Good Judgment Project research on forecasting accuracy.

These aren't universal deployments. Most organizations that try internal markets face adoption challenges. The cultural requirement — that people trade honestly rather than to signal alignment with management — is often the harder problem to solve than the technical one.

Illustrative. Internal prediction markets can be applied to project delivery, sales, product, and competitive intelligence questions.

When to use them vs. when not to

Good fitPoor fit
Quantifiable, verifiable outcome (a date, a revenue number, a launch)Ambiguous or subjective outcome (quality of a product, cultural fit)
Dispersed information across a team (many people have partial views)One expert with comprehensive information (market adds noise, not signal)
Psychological safety to trade honestly (against management's official view)Culture where trading against official forecasts is risky
Enough participants to form a real market (20+ traders)Small team where any trade is identifiable to leadership

The conditions matter. A poorly designed internal market — with inadequate anonymity, insufficient participation, or a culture where betting against management is career risk — will produce worse forecasts than just asking people directly.

External prediction markets as business intelligence

Even without running internal markets, public platforms like Kalshi list economically relevant questions: interest rate decisions, inflation data releases, regulatory outcomes. These are real probability estimates from markets with financial stakes. For business planning that depends on those variables, they're a more direct signal than consensus analyst forecasts.

Frequently asked questions

What is an internal prediction market?
A market where employees trade contracts on questions relevant to the organization's operations — project timelines, revenue targets, product launches. The price reflects aggregate employee belief, not official management forecasts.
Do internal prediction markets actually improve forecast accuracy?
Research and practitioner experience suggest they can, particularly for questions where information is dispersed across the organization and official forecasts are systematically biased. They require appropriate design and culture to work.
What companies have used prediction markets?
Google ran internal prediction markets for years. Hewlett-Packard ran internal markets for sales forecasting. Various intelligence agencies have used prediction market approaches for geopolitical forecasting. Enterprise platforms like Cultivate Labs serve corporate clients today.
What makes an internal prediction market fail?
Most often, culture. When employees believe trading against official forecasts carries career risk, prices converge to official forecasts rather than honest beliefs. The mechanism only works with genuine anonymity and genuine safety to dissent.
