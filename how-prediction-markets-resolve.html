Contract Mechanics

The question closes. Someone gets $1. Here's exactly how that happens.

Resolution is where prediction markets turn probability into money. The resolution criteria are the most important thing to read before you trade. Here's why.

Published December 11, 2026

The basic payout structure

Most prediction market contracts pay $1 per share to the winning side and $0 to the losing side. If you hold YES shares in a contract and it resolves YES, each share is worth $1. If it resolves NO, they're worth $0.

The price you paid for those shares determines your profit or loss. If you bought YES at $0.72 and it resolves YES, you gain $0.28 per share. If it resolves NO, you lose $0.72 per share.

What "resolution criteria" means

Every contract specifies the exact conditions under which it resolves YES or NO. This is not fine print. It's the core of the contract.

Consider a hypothetical contract: "Will Candidate X win the presidential election?" At first glance that seems clear. But the criteria need to answer: When exactly does it resolve? Based on which source? What if the result is disputed? What if there's a recount? What if no winner is called by the resolution date?

Platforms spell this out in the contract's resolution rules. The difference between two similar contracts from two different platforms often comes down to these details, which is part of why prices diverge across platforms. See Polymarket vs. Kalshi price comparison for context.

Platform judgment calls happen when the event is ambiguous, delayed, or not covered by the initial criteria. Both Kalshi and Polymarket reserve the right to resolve contracts through their own processes when standard criteria don't apply cleanly.

What happens when it's ambiguous

Ambiguous resolution is the scenario prediction market traders lose most sleep over. It happens when a real-world outcome doesn't map cleanly onto YES or NO.

In these cases, platforms typically resolve at $0.50 (treating it as a coin flip between both sides), cancel the market and refund participants, or exercise admin discretion to call YES or NO based on the spirit of the contract.

Which path a platform takes depends on its published resolution policy. Reading that policy before placing a trade in a contract with any potential for ambiguity is not optional.

Illustrative. Ambiguous outcomes follow different resolution paths depending on platform policy.

The practical rule: Before entering a position in any contract, read the full resolution criteria. If you can imagine a real-world scenario where it's unclear whether the event resolves YES or NO, check how the platform handles that edge case in its policy documentation.

Timing: when does a contract close?

Contracts have an expiration date and time, after which new trading stops. Resolution happens after that — sometimes immediately, sometimes days later, depending on when the data source confirms the outcome.

Between expiration and resolution, the contract is in a "pending" state. Your shares are locked. You can't sell. When the resolution is confirmed, payouts process automatically.

Frequently asked questions

How does a prediction market contract resolve?
Based on the specific criteria stated in the contract. When the triggering condition is confirmed by the designated source, YES holders receive $1 per share and NO holders receive $0, or vice versa.
What happens if the outcome is disputed or delayed?
Platforms handle this differently. Common outcomes include resolving at $0.50, canceling the market and refunding participants, or exercising admin discretion. Check the platform's specific resolution policy.
Can I exit a contract before it resolves?
Yes, as long as the market is still open (before the expiration date). Once the market closes, you hold your position to resolution.
Why do two platforms have different prices for the same question?
Often because the resolution criteria differ in meaningful ways. Even a small difference in the triggering condition can justify a large price difference between platforms.
