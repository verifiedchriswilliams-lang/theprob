Market Mechanics

The number is 80%. The market has 3 trades. Here's why that matters.

Liquidity is the context behind prediction market prices. Ignore it and you'll misread signals. Understand it and you'll know when a probability means something.

Published November 27, 2026

Liquidity is not volume

Volume measures how much trading has happened, total. Liquidity describes how easily you can buy or sell contracts right now without moving the price. They sound similar. They're not.

A market can have $10 million in historical volume and barely any depth today. Another market might have lower cumulative volume but a healthy live order book. The second one is more useful for reading a current signal. For background on how prediction market prices work in general, see the guide on how to read prediction market odds.

Illustrative comparison. Volume is historical. Liquidity is current.

The bid-ask spread

The bid is the highest price a buyer will pay right now. The ask is the lowest price a seller will accept. The gap between them is the spread. Cross it and you pay an immediate trading cost.

OrderExample price
Best YES bid$0.48
Best YES ask$0.52
Spread$0.04 (4 percentage points)

Buy immediately at $0.52. Sell immediately at $0.48. The 4-cent gap is gone the moment you transact. A narrow spread means better trading conditions. A wide spread means the market is less liquid and the displayed midpoint might not be real for any meaningful order size.

Illustrative order book. Real spreads vary by market and platform.

Market depth: how much can actually trade?

Depth describes how many contracts are available at or near the current price. If 1,000 contracts sit at $0.50 but the next 10,000 are only available at $0.65, a large buyer will push the price upward fast.

The last traded price is not the price at which a large order executes. This is why a $10 million lifetime volume number tells you very little about what happens when you try to buy $5,000 of contracts today.

Illustrative. A deep market absorbs large orders with minimal price impact. A thin market does not.

Why thin markets mislead

Two markets both show YES at 70%. Market A: active, narrow spread, real depth. Market B: 4 open orders, wide spread. A moderate buy in Market B could push it to 82%. Not because the event got more likely. Because the market was thin.

A dramatic price move in a thin market is more noise than signal. A 20-point shift in a deep, high-volume market usually means something happened.

This matters especially when comparing prices across platforms. For a full look at cross-platform price differences, see the guide on Polymarket vs. Kalshi price comparison.

Does more volume mean better forecasts?

Not automatically. High volume attracts more participants and makes prices harder to manipulate with a small trade. That supports price discovery. But it doesn't guarantee informed participants or accurate outcomes.

High-volume markets have been wrong. Lightly traded markets have been right. Volume is a useful proxy for reliability, not a guarantee of it. For a deeper look at what the research says, read are prediction markets accurate.

A quick liquidity checklist

Before treating a price as a strong signal: Is the market actively trading today, not just historically? Is the spread narrow? Is there real depth near the displayed price? Did the price move alongside meaningful volume? Are comparable markets on other platforms moving the same way?

Frequently asked questions

What does liquidity mean in prediction markets?
How easily you can buy or sell contracts without materially moving the price. A liquid market has depth, narrow spreads, and active trading. An illiquid one doesn't.
Is volume the same as liquidity?
No. Volume is past trading activity. Liquidity is the current ability to transact near the market price. High historical volume doesn't mean the market is liquid right now.
What is a bid-ask spread?
The difference between the best available buying price and the best available selling price. A narrow spread means lower trading costs and better market conditions.
Does high volume guarantee accuracy?
No. It may support price discovery, but it doesn't guarantee a correct forecast. Volume is one input, not a verdict.
How do I know if a prediction market is liquid enough to trust?
Check recent trading activity, bid-ask spread, and whether the price moved on actual volume. Compare with other platforms covering the same event. If all signs point to thin trading, treat the price as tentative.
